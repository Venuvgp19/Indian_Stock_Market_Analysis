📚 Trading Strategies Explained

1. SMA Crossover Strategy

How it works: Uses two Simple Moving Averages (SMA) - a short-term (20-day) and long-term (50-day).

Example - RELIANCE.NS:

  • 20-day SMA = Rs. 2,400 (short-term trend)
  • 50-day SMA = Rs. 2,350 (long-term trend)
  • SIGNAL: BUY - 20-day crosses above 50-day (Golden Cross)
  • Later: 20-day drops below 50-day → SELL (Death Cross)

✅ Best for: Trending markets (strong up or down moves)

Medium Risk

2. RSI Strategy

How it works: RSI (Relative Strength Index) ranges from 0-100. Buy when oversold, sell when overbought.

Example:

  • RSI drops to 28 → BUY (oversold, expect bounce back)
  • RSI rises to 72 → SELL (overbought, expect pullback)
  • RSI between 30-70 → HOLD (neutral zone)

✅ Best for: Range-bound markets (moving sideways)

Medium Risk

3. MACD Strategy

How it works: MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) shows momentum shifts.

Example:

  • MACD line = 12, Signal line = 10 → BUY (bullish crossover)
  • MACD line = 8, Signal line = 11 → SELL (bearish crossover)
  • Histogram turning positive = Increasing momentum

✅ Best for: Capturing momentum shifts early

Medium Risk

4. Bollinger Bands Strategy

How it works: Uses upper and lower bands around a moving average. Buy at lower band, sell at upper band.

Example:

  • Price hits Lower Band (Rs. 2,300) → BUY (oversold, expect mean reversion)
  • Price hits Upper Band (Rs. 2,600) → SELL (overbought)
  • Price in middle → HOLD (no clear signal)

✅ Best for: Mean reversion trading (buying dips, selling peaks)

Low-Medium Risk

5. Combined Strategy ⭐ (RECOMMENDED)

How it works: Uses ALL indicators together. Only trades when most indicators agree (3 out of 4).

Example:

  • SMA: Bullish ✓
  • RSI: 45 (Neutral) ✓
  • MACD: Bullish ✓
  • Bollinger: Middle zone ✓
  • Result: BUY (3/4 indicators bullish)

✅ Best for: Reducing false signals and avoiding whipsaws

Low Risk

Note: Requires at least 3 indicators to agree before trading. Less frequent but more reliable signals.

6. ADX Trend Strength Strategy

How it works: Uses Average Directional Index (ADX) to measure trend strength. Only trades when trend is strong enough.

Example:

  • ADX = 32 (above 25 threshold) +DI (14) crosses above -DI (10) → BUY (strong bullish trend)
  • ADX = 18 (weak trend) → HOLD (no trade, trend too weak)
  • ADX = 30, -DI crosses above +DI → SELL (strong bearish trend)

✅ Best for: Avoiding trades during weak/ranging markets

Medium Risk

7. SuperTrend Strategy

How it works: Uses ATR-based trend indicator. Green line below price = buy, red line above price = sell.

Example:

  • Price (Rs. 2,500) above SuperTrend (Rs. 2,450) → BUY (uptrend confirmed)
  • Price breaks below SuperTrend → SELL (downtrend starts)
  • Works as dynamic trailing stop-loss

✅ Best for: Trend-following with automatic stop-loss

Medium Risk

8. Ichimoku Cloud Strategy

How it works: Complex Japanese indicator. Price above cloud + Tenkan > Kijun = buy. Price below cloud = sell.

Example:

  • Price above Senkou Span A & B (Cloud) + Tenkan (9-day) > Kijun (26-day) → BUY
  • Price inside Cloud → HOLD (neutral zone)
  • Price below Cloud + Tenkan < Kijun → SELL

✅ Best for: Mid-to-long term trend identification

Medium Risk

9. Breakout Strategy

How it works: Buys when price breaks above recent highs, sells when breaks below recent lows.

Example:

  • 20-day high = Rs. 2,500, Price breaks above → BUY (momentum breakout)
  • Price keeps making higher highs → HOLD
  • Price drops below 20-day low (Rs. 2,300) → SELL (breakdown)

✅ Best for: Catching strong momentum moves early

High Risk

Note: Can produce false breakouts. Use volume confirmation when possible.

10. Mean Reversion Strategy

How it works: Opposite of trend-following. Buys when price drops below statistical mean, sells when above.

Example:

  • 20-day mean = Rs. 2,400, 2σ lower band = Rs. 2,200
  • Price hits Rs. 2,180 (below lower band) → BUY (expect bounce back to mean)
  • Price hits Rs. 2,620 (above upper band) → SELL

✅ Best for: Range-bound, non-trending stocks

High Risk

Warning: Can cause losses if stock enters a strong trend. Don't fight the trend.

11. Momentum Strategy (ROC)

How it works: Uses Rate of Change (ROC) to compare short-term vs long-term momentum.

Example:

  • 12-day ROC = +5%, 26-day ROC = +2% → BUY (short-term momentum accelerating)
  • 12-day ROC = -3%, 26-day ROC = +1% → SELL (momentum slowing)
  • Buy strongest momentum, sell weakest

✅ Best for: Momentum traders following relative strength

High Risk

12. Multi-Timeframe Strategy

How it works: Uses 3 EMAs + SMA. All moving averages aligned = strongest signal.

Example:

  • EMA-5 > EMA-10 > EMA-20 > SMA-50 → BUY (all timeframes bullish)
  • EMA-5 < EMA-10 < EMA-20 → SELL (3/4 bearish)
  • Mixed alignment → HOLD (conflicting signals)

✅ Best for: Trend confirmation across multiple timeframes

Low Risk

Note: More conservative than single timeframe. Waits for strong trend confirmation.

📊 Understanding Results Table

Total Return

The overall profit/loss percentage from the strategy. Example: +25% means Rs. 100 became Rs. 125.

Buy & Hold Return

How much you'd make by simply buying and holding the stock (benchmark). Example: If stock went from Rs. 100 to Rs. 120, this is 20%.

Outperformance

How much better (or worse) the strategy did vs. simply holding. Example: +5% means strategy beat buy-and-hold by 5 percentage points.

Sharpe Ratio

Risk-adjusted return measure. Higher is better. Above 1.0 is good, above 2.0 is excellent. Example: 1.5 means you got good returns for the risk taken.

Max Drawdown

Worst losing streak from peak to trough. Example: -15% means the strategy lost 15% at its worst before recovering. Lower is better.

Win Rate

Percentage of profitable trades. Example: 55% means 55 out of 100 trades were profitable. Higher is better but not everything (size of wins matters too).

Total Trades

Number of buy/sell transactions. Example: 24 trades means the strategy bought and sold 12 times. Fewer trades = lower brokerage costs.

💡 Pro Tips

  • Start with Combined Strategy - safest option requiring multiple confirmations
  • Use 2+ years of data for reliable results (includes different market conditions)
  • Compare strategies on the same stock to find what works best for that particular stock
  • Higher win rate + positive Sharpe = Good strategy
  • Max drawdown below -20% = Manageable risk
